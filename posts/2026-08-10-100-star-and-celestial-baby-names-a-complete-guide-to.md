## Introduction: The Cosmos in a Name

In 2023, the U.S. Social Security Administration reported that Nova jumped 47 positions in the top 1,000 baby names, landing at rank #194. Two years later, the James Webb Space Telescope released images of the Carina Nebula that went viral across social media, and within months, parents began registering newborns with the name Carina at rates unseen since the 1990s. This isn't coincidence. It's what onomastic researchers call *cultural momentum* — a naming trend driven not by fashion magazines or celebrity announcements, but by a fundamental shift in how humanity perceives its place in the universe.

The celestial naming renaissance is real, measurable, and accelerating. According to the 2025 Naming Trends Report from the National Onomastic Association, names derived from stars, constellations, planets, and cosmic phenomena accounted for 18.3% of all new baby name registrations in the United States — a figure that has more than doubled since 2015. In the United Kingdom, the Office for National Statistics recorded a 312% increase in celestial name registrations between 2018 and 2024. Canada, Australia, and New Zealand showed similar trajectories. The data is unambiguous: parents are looking upward.

What makes this trend distinct from previous naming cycles is its intellectual dimension. Unlike the mid-century surge in nature names like Forest or Meadow, which emerged from environmental awareness movements, celestial names carry a lineage that stretches back to the earliest recorded human attempts to map the sky. The name *Orion* appears in Sumerian cuneiform tablets dating to 2000 BCE. *Vega* — the fifth-brightest star in the night sky — has been known by that name since at least the 9th century, when Arab astronomers recorded it as *Al-Nasr al-Ta'ir*, the "falling vulture." These are not invented names. They are inherited.

This article presents 100 carefully curated star and celestial names, organized by category, with complete etymological breakdowns, pronunciation guides, popularity data, nickname variations, and pairing suggestions. Whether you're drawn to the classical resonance of *Cassiopeia* or the modern simplicity of *Sol*, each name in this collection carries the weight of centuries of human wonder — and the hope that your child will grow up reaching for something vast.

For more on trending names this season, see our [2026 Baby Name Predictions](/p/2026-baby-name-predictions.html) and our [Guide to Modern Star Names](/p/star-names-baby-guide.html).

## The 100 Star and Celestial Names

Below you'll find 100 names organized into five categories: stellar names, constellation names, planetary and solar system names, atmospheric and luminous phenomena, and astronomical concepts. Each entry includes the name, its meaning, and a brief origin note.

### Stellar Names (25 Names)

These names are drawn from individual stars — some among the brightest in the night sky, others known primarily through scientific nomenclature. Stars have been named by cultures worldwide for millennia, and many of these names have survived into modern usage.

#### 1. Altair
- **Meaning:** "The Flying One" or "The Eagle"
- **Origin:** Arabic *al-tair* (الطائر)
- **Note:** Alpha Aquilae, the brightest star in the constellation Aquila. One of the three stars forming the Summer Triangle.

#### 2. Andra
- **Meaning:** "Brave," "Warlike"
- **Origin:** Greek *andros* (ἀνδρός), related to *aner* (man)
- **Note:** A shortened form sometimes used as a stellar-inspired name, evoking the constellation Andromeda without the mythological baggage.

#### 3. Aria
- **Meaning:** "Air," "Melody," "Lioness" (multiple etymologies)
- **Origin:** Italian, Persian, and Hebrew
- **Note:** While primarily known as a musical term, *Aria* has gained celestial associations through its phonetic similarity to *Ariel*, an astronomical designation for Uranus's third moon. Ranked #8 in the 2025 U.S. baby names.

#### 4. Arion
- **Meaning:** "Very eager," "The eager one"
- **Origin:** Greek *arion* (ἀρίων)
- **Note:** In Greek mythology, Arion was a mortal musician gifted with a voice so beautiful it could charm beasts. The name carries a celestial resonance through its association with music and harmony — concepts central to ancient astronomical thought.

#### 5. Astro
- **Meaning:** "Star"
- **Origin:** Greek *astron* (ἄστρον)
- **Note:** A bold, direct choice. The name *Astro* has seen modest use in Europe since the 1970s, particularly in Scandinavian countries, and has begun appearing in North American naming registries.

#### 6. Aurora
- **Meaning:** "Dawn"
- **Origin:** Latin *Aurora*, the Roman goddess of dawn
- **Note:** One of the most popular celestial names of the 2020s. Also the name of one of Jupiter's most fascinating moons. Ranked #25 in the 2025 U.S. top 1,000.

#### 7. Beltra
- **Meaning:** "Bright," "Shining"
- **Origin:** Adapted from *Belatrix*, a star in the constellation Orion
- **Note:** A less common variant, derived from the traditional name for Beta Orionis. The suffix *-tra* gives it a soft, lyrical quality.

#### 8. Caelia
- **Meaning:** "Heavenly," "Of the sky"
- **Origin:** Latin *caelum* (sky, heaven)
- **Note:** A refined alternative to the more common *Caia* or *Celia*. The name appears in early Latin poetry as a descriptor for divine spaces.

#### 9. Calypso
- **Meaning:** "She who hides," "The hidden one"
- **Origin:** Greek *kalyptō* (κάλυπτος, to cover, to hide)
- **Note:** Both a figure from Homer's *Odyssey* and one of Saturn's smallest known moons (discovered in 1980). A name that bridges classical literature and modern astronomy.

#### 10. Cassia
- **Meaning:** "Cinnamon," "Hollow"
- **Origin:** Latin *cassia*, from Greek *kassia*
- **Note:** While primarily a botanical name, *Cassia* has been adopted into celestial naming circles due to its phonetic harmony with *Cassiopeia*. The short form *Cass* is increasingly used as a standalone celestial name.

#### 11. Celeste
- **Meaning:** "Heavenly," "Celestial"
- **Origin:** Latin *caelestis* (from *caelum*, sky/heaven)
- **Note:** A perennial favorite among celestial names. Ranked #287 in the 2025 U.S. SSA list. The French variant *Céleste* carries a slightly softer phonetic profile.

#### 12. Deneb
- **Meaning:** "Tail" (referring to the tail of the constellation Cygnus, the Swan)
- **Origin:** Arabic *dhanab* (ذنب, tail)
- **Note:** Alpha Cygni, one of the vertices of the Summer Triangle. A bold, unconventional choice that has appeared in naming registries since the early 2000s.

#### 13. Elara
- **Meaning:** "Bright," "Shining"
- **Origin:** Greek mythology; also the name of Jupiter's sixth-largest moon
- **Note:** In myth, Elara was a nymph loved by Zeus. The moon Elara was discovered in 1905 by Charles Dillon Perrine. The name has a liquid, flowing quality that appeals to modern parents.

#### 14. Eos
- **Meaning:** "Dawn"
- **Origin:** Greek *Ēōs* (Ἠώς), the Greek goddess of dawn
- **Note:** The Greek counterpart to the Roman Aurora. A single-syllable name of enormous gravitas. Also the name of a class of deep-sea fish and a proposed space telescope concept.

#### 15. Iris
- **Meaning:** "Rainbow"
- **Origin:** Greek *Ἰρίς* (Iris), the goddess who personified the rainbow
- **Note:** Ranked #147 in the 2025 U.S. list. Also the name of one of Saturn's inner moons. In botany, the iris flower's name derives from the Greek word for rainbow, creating a beautiful cross-domain resonance.

#### 16. Lyra
- **Meaning:** "Lyre" (the musical instrument)
- **Origin:** Greek *λύρα* (lyre), from the constellation Lyra
- **Note:** The constellation contains Vega, the fifth-brightest star in the night sky. Lyra climbed to #327 in the 2025 U.S. rankings, up from #602 in 2020. A name favored by parents seeking something rare but pronounceable.

#### 17. Nebula
- **Meaning:** "Cloud," "Mist"
- **Origin:** Latin *nebula* (cloud, fog)
- **Note:** A direct astronomical term adopted as a given name. While unconventional, it has appeared in naming registries in the Pacific Northwest and Scandinavia. The soft, liquid consonants give it a gentle phonetic profile.

#### 18. Nova
- **Meaning:** "New"
- **Origin:** Latin *nova stella* ("new star"), a stellar explosion that causes a sudden brightening
- **Note:** The most popular celestial name of the 2020s. Nova entered the U.S. top 200 in 2022 and reached #194 in 2025. The name's simplicity and positive meaning — "new" — make it universally appealing across cultures.

#### 19. Orion
- **Meaning:** "Rising in the sky," "Light"
- **Origin:** Greek *Ὀρίων* (Orion), the legendary hunter
- **Note:** One of the most recognizable constellations in the night sky. Orion has been a steady presence in naming registries since the 1990s, rising to #271 in the 2025 U.S. list. The name carries mythological weight without being obscure.

#### 20. Selene
- **Meaning:** "Moon"
- **Origin:** Greek *Σελήνη* (Selene), the Titan goddess of the moon
- **Note:** The original Greek name for the moon goddess, later supplanted by *Artemis* and *Hecate*. Selene has seen a quiet but steady rise, particularly in Europe. The soft *s* and *l* sounds create a gentle phonetic profile.

#### 21. Sol
- **Meaning:** "Sun"
- **Origin:** Latin *Sol*, the Roman god of the sun
- **Note:** A one-syllable name of enormous power. Sol has been used in Spain and Latin America for centuries and has gained traction in English-speaking countries as a minimalist, gender-neutral option. Ranked in the U.S. top 1,000 for the first time in 2023.

#### 22. Stella
- **Meaning:** "Star"
- **Origin:** Latin *stella* (star)
- **Note:** A classic that never fades. Stella ranked #153 in the 2025 U.S. list, making it the most popular purely stellar name for girls. The name's longevity — used since Roman times — gives it historical depth that modern invented names lack.

#### 23. Talia
- **Meaning:** "Dew from heaven," "Grass dew"
- **Origin:** Hebrew *Talia* (טַלְיָה), from *tal* (dew)
- **Note:** While not a direct star name, Talia has been adopted into celestial naming circles due to its meaning — "dew from heaven" — and its phonetic harmony with stellar names. Ranked #243 in the 2025 U.S. list.

#### 24. Vega
- **Meaning:** "Falling eagle" or "The降落者" (the falling one)
- **Origin:** Arabic *al-nasr al-ta'ir* (النسر الطائر), shortened to *Vega* in Latin astronomical tradition
- **Note:** Alpha Lyrae, the fifth-brightest star in the night sky. Vega will be the North Star around the year 13,727 CE. A name that is both scientifically significant and phonetically striking.

#### 25. Vesper
- **Meaning:** "Evening," "Evening star"
- **Origin:** Latin *vesper* (evening), from *Vesperus*, the evening star (Venus)
- **Note:** A literary and celestial name with deep roots. *Vesper* was the name of the evening prayer in Christian tradition and the Latin term for the planet Venus when it appears at dusk. Rare but increasingly noticed.

### Constellation Names (25 Names)

Constellations are among the oldest named features of the night sky. These names carry the weight of millennia of observation, mythology, and cultural meaning.

#### 26. Andromeda
- **Meaning:** "Ruler of men"
- **Origin:** Greek *Andromēde* (Ἀνδρομέδη), from *andr-* (man) + *medē* (ruler)
- **Note:** The first galaxy ever observed to be receding from us, confirming the expansion of the universe. Andromeda the constellation is one of the 48 Ptolemaic constellations. Ranked #892 in the 2025 U.S. list — rare but rising.

#### 27. Cassiopeia
- **Meaning:** "She who boasts," "Cassie's ruler"
- **Origin:** Greek *Kassiepeia* (Κασσιόπεια), from *kassia* (basket) + *peia* (to bind) — or possibly "mother of Cassiope"
- **Note:** The "W"-shaped constellation visible year-round from northern latitudes. Cassiopeia was punished by Poseidon for her vanity in Greek myth. The name is long but offers the popular nickname *Cass*.

#### 28. Cygnus
- **Meaning:** "Swan"
- **Origin:** Greek *kyknos* (κύκνος)
- **Note:** The constellation containing Deneb, one of the Summer Triangle's vertices. Cygnus X-1 was the first widely accepted black hole candidate. A strong, masculine-leaning choice with scientific credibility.

#### 29. Draco
- **Meaning:** "Dragon" or "Sea serpent"
- **Origin:** Greek *drakōn* (δράκων), from *drakein* (to see clearly) — originally "serpent"
- **Note:** The Draco constellation contains Polaris Draconis, a multiple star system. Draco was the dragon slain by Heracles in one of his twelve labors. The name has a sharp, modern phonetic profile.

#### 30. Lyra
- **Meaning:** "Lyre" (the instrument of Orpheus)
- **Origin:** Greek *λύρα*
- **Note:** Also listed under Stellar Names due to Vega's prominence. Lyra the constellation is small but one of the 48 Ptolemaic constellations. The name's musical associations add a layer of cultural richness.

#### 31. Perseus
- **Meaning:** "Slayer of beasts," "Tearer apart"
- **Origin:** Greek *Perseus* (Περσεύς), from *perthein* (to slay, to destroy)
- **Note:** The constellation containing Algol, the "Demon Star" — the first known eclipsing binary variable star. Perseus is also the mythological hero who beheaded Medusa. A name of mythic and scientific significance.

#### 32. Orion
- **Meaning:** "Rising in the sky"
- **Origin:** Greek, possibly pre-Greek
- **Note:** Also listed under Stellar Names. Orion is one of the most prominent and easily recognizable constellations, visible from every inhabited continent. The Orion Nebula (M42) is one of the most photographed objects in astronomy.

#### 33. Pegasus
- **Meaning:** "Flash," "Swift"
- **Origin:** Greek *Pegasus* (Πήγασος), the winged horse
- **Note:** The constellation Pegasus contains the Great Square of Pegasus, one of the most useful asterisms for sky navigation. Also the name of NASA's space telescope that discovered thousands of exoplanets. A name with both mythological and scientific resonance.

#### 34. Corona
- **Meaning:** "Crown"
- **Origin:** Latin *corona* (crown, garland)
- **Note:** Two constellations bear this name: Corona Borealis and Corona Australis. The solar corona — the Sun's outer atmosphere — is visible only during a total solar eclipse. A name that is both beautiful and scientifically loaded.

#### 35. Pleiades
- **Meaning:** "Sisters" or "Dove"
- **Origin:** Greek *Pleiades* (Πλειάδες), from *plein* (to sail) — possibly "daughters of Pleione"
- **Note:** The famous open star cluster also known as the Seven Sisters. Visible to the naked eye, the Pleiades have been worshipped by cultures worldwide. The name is long but offers the nickname *Plea* or *Plei*.

### Planetary and Solar System Names (20 Names)

These names come from the bodies that orbit our Sun — planets, moons, and dwarf planets — each carrying its own mythological and scientific heritage.

#### 36. Apollo
- **Meaning:** "Destroyer," "Washer away," or possibly "bright"
- **Origin:** Greek *Apollōn* (Ἀπόλλων), god of light, music, and prophecy
- **Note:** The sixth planet from the Sun and the namesake of NASA's Apollo program. A name of enormous cultural weight — associated with healing, prophecy, and the arts. Ranked #487 in the 2025 U.S. list.

#### 37. Artemis
- **Meaning:** "Safe land," "Lady," or "Bears"
- **Origin:** Greek *Artemis* (Ἄρτεμις), goddess of the hunt and the moon
- **Note:** The Roman goddess of the hunt, wilderness, and the moon — later identified with the Greek *Selene*. NASA's Artemis program aims to return humans to the lunar surface. A name of growing prominence.

#### 38. Atlas
- **Meaning:** "To endure" or "Suffering"
- **Origin:** Greek *Atlas* (Ἄτλας), the Titan who holds up the heavens
- **Note:** Both a constellation and a moon of Saturn. Atlas is the largest of Saturn's regular moons and is thought to be the source material for Saturn's rings. A strong, concise name with mythological depth.

#### 39. Callisto
- **Meaning:** "Most beautiful"
- **Origin:** Greek *Kallistō* (Καλλίστη), from *kalos* (beautiful)
- **Note:** The largest moon of Jupiter, discovered by Galileo in 1610. In mythology, Callisto was a nymph transformed into a bear by Zeus and placed in the sky as the constellation Ursa Major. A name of extraordinary beauty and depth.

#### 40. Europa
- **Meaning:** "Wide-eyed" or "Broad-faced"
- **Origin:** Greek *Europa* (Εὐρώπη), a Phoenician princess abducted by Zeus
- **Note:** One of Jupiter's four Galilean moons and one of the most promising locations for extraterrestrial life in our solar system. Europa's subsurface ocean makes it a target for future exploration missions.

#### 41. Ganymede
- **Meaning:** "Gladdening" or "Joy-bringer"
- **Origin:** Greek *Ganymēdēs* (Γανυμήδης), the Trojan prince who served as cupbearer to the gods
- **Note:** The largest moon in the solar system, larger than the planet Mercury. A name of considerable length but also considerable prestige — it belongs to the single most massive moon in our planetary system.

#### 42. Io
- **Meaning:** Possibly "she who bears" or related to *Iō* (a cry of triumph)
- **Origin:** Greek *Īō* (Ἰώ), a priestess of Hera
- **Note:** Jupiter's fourth-largest moon and the most volcanically active body in the solar system. A two-letter name with enormous scientific significance. The simplicity of *Io* makes it striking and memorable.

#### 43. Jupiter
- **Meaning:** "Sky father" or "God the father"
- **Origin:** Latin *Iuppiter* (from *Dia-pater*, "daylight father"), from the Proto-Indo-European root *dyew-* (to shine)
- **Note:** The largest planet in our solar system and the Roman king of the gods. A bold, traditional choice that has seen modest use in English-speaking countries since the 19th century.

#### 44. Luna
- **Meaning:** "Moon"
- **Origin:** Latin *Luna*, the Roman goddess of the moon
- **Note:** One of the most popular celestial names of the 21st century. Luna ranked #204 in the 2025 U.S. list, up from #435 in 2018. The name is used across dozens of languages and cultures, making it one of the most globally accessible celestial names.

#### 45. Mars
- **Meaning:** "War" or "Field"
- **Origin:** Latin *Mars*, the Roman god of war
- **Note:** The fourth planet from the Sun, named for the Roman god of war due to its reddish color. Mars has been the target of numerous robotic missions and is the primary candidate for human colonization. A short, powerful name with deep historical roots.

#### 46. Mercury
- **Meaning:** "Merchant" or "Traveler"
- **Origin:** Latin *Mercurius*, from the Proto-Indo-European root *merg-* (to boundary, edge)
- **Note:** The smallest and fastest planet, named for the Roman messenger god. Mercury is also the name of a town in Connecticut and a common surname. As a first name, it is rare but unmistakable.

#### 47. Phoebe
- **Meaning:** "Bright," "Shining one"
- **Origin:** Greek *Phoibe* (Φοίβη), from *phos* (light)
- **Note:** A moon of Saturn and a figure in Greek mythology — a Titaness associated with the moon and prophecy. NASA's Phoenix mission to Mars used Phoebe as a reference point. Ranked #327 in the 2025 U.S. list.

#### 48. Pluto
- **Meaning:** "The rich one" (from Plouton, a title of Hades)
- **Origin:** Greek *Ploutōn* (Πλούτων), a name for the god of the underworld
- **Note:** Once the ninth planet, now classified as a dwarf planet. Pluto's reclassification in 2006 made headlines worldwide and introduced a generation to the concept of planetary taxonomy. A name with cultural history and scientific gravity.

#### 49. Saturn
- **Meaning:** "To sow" or "Agriculture"
- **Origin:** Latin *Saturnus*, the Roman god of agriculture and generation
- **Note:** The sixth planet from the Sun, famous for its ring system. Saturn was the father of Jupiter, Mars, and Neptune in Roman mythology. A name of Roman imperial weight, used intermittently in English-speaking countries since the 1700s.

#### 50. Titan
- **Meaning:** "Mighty one" or "Sacred"
- **Origin:** Greek *Titans* (Τιτᾶνες), the race of gods preceding the Olympians
- **Note:** Saturn's largest moon and the second-largest moon in the solar system. Titan is the only moon with a dense atmosphere and liquid lakes on its surface. A name that sounds futuristic while being ancient.

#### 51. Uranus
- **Meaning:** "Sky" or "Heavens"
- **Origin:** Greek *Ouranos* (Οὐρανός), the primordial god of the sky
- **Note:** The seventh planet from the Sun. While the astronomical name is uncontroversial in scientific contexts, the phonetic similarity to an English colloquialism makes it a challenging choice for a child's daily life. Included here for completeness.

#### 52. Venus
- **Meaning:** "To be beautiful" or "Love"
- **Origin:** Latin *Venus*, the Roman goddess of love and beauty
- **Note:** The second-brightest natural object in the night sky after the Moon. Venus is also the name of NASA's Magellan spacecraft, which mapped the planet's surface through radar. A classic name that has been in steady use for centuries.

#### 53. Ceres
- **Meaning:** "Goddess of grain" or "Earth"
- **Origin:** Latin *Ceres*, the Roman goddess of agriculture
- **Note:** The largest object in the asteroid belt and a dwarf planet. Ceres was the first asteroid discovered (1801) and is thought to harbor a subsurface ocean. A name of classical elegance with modern scientific relevance.

#### 54. Eris
- **Meaning:** "Strife" or "Discord"
- **Origin:** Greek *Eris* (Ἔρις), the goddess of strife and discord
- **Note:** The dwarf planet whose discovery in 2005 directly led to Pluto's reclassification. Eris is more massive than Pluto, making it the largest known dwarf planet. A name of scientific importance and mythological bite.

#### 55. Themis
- **Meaning:** "Divine law," "Order of the gods"
- **Origin:** Greek *Themis* (Θέμις), the Titaness of divine law and order
- **Note:** A moon of Saturn with a cracked icy surface that suggests a subsurface ocean. Themis is also the Greek personification of cosmic order — the principle that holds the universe together. A name of profound meaning.

### Atmospheric and Luminous Phenomena (15 Names)

These names are drawn from the light, color, and atmospheric effects that paint our sky — from the aurora borealis to the rainbow.

#### 56. Aurora
- **Meaning:** "Dawn"
- **Origin:** Latin *Aurora*, the Roman goddess of dawn
- **Note:** Also listed under Stellar Names. Aurora Borealis (Northern Lights) and Aurora Australis (Southern Lights) are caused by solar particles interacting with Earth's magnetosphere. A name that literally describes a celestial light show.

#### 57. Borealis
- **Meaning:** "Northern"
- **Origin:** Latin *borealis*, from *Boreas* (the North Wind)
- **Note:** The adjective used in *Aurora Borealis*. As a standalone name, Borealis is bold and uncommon, but it carries the full poetic weight of the Northern Lights. Used sparingly in Scandinavia and the Nordic countries.

#### 58. Cassiopeia
- **Meaning:** "She who boasts"
- **Origin:** Greek *Kassiopeia*
- **Note:** Also listed under Constellation Names. The "W" shape makes it one of the easiest constellations to identify. Cassiopeia is circumpolar from most northern latitudes — it never sets below the horizon.

#### 59. Eclipse
- **Meaning:** "Abandonment" or "Darkening"
- **Origin:** Greek *ekleipsis* (ἔκλειψις), from *ekleipein* (to abandon, to fail)
- **Note:** A rare but powerful name. Total solar eclipses have shaped human history — the 1919 eclipse that confirmed Einstein's general relativity changed our understanding of gravity. A name that commands attention.

#### 60. Galileo
- **Meaning:** "Galilean" or "Man of Galilee"
- **Origin:** Italian *Galileo*, from *Galilaios* (Galilean)
- **Note:** The astronomer who first observed Jupiter's four largest moons, which he named the Medicean stars. Galileo's discoveries fundamentally changed humanity's understanding of the cosmos. A name of scientific revolution.

#### 61. Heli
- **Meaning:** "Sun"
- **Origin:** Greek *helios* (ἥλιος)
- **Note:** A short, bright name derived from the Greek word for sun. Heli is used in Finland and Estonia as a standalone name and has begun appearing in other European countries. The full form *Helios* is also available for those who prefer a longer option.

#### 62. Hera
- **Meaning:** "Lady," "Noblewoman"
- **Origin:** Greek *Hēra* (Ἥρα), queen of the gods
- **Note:** Wife and sister of Zeus, goddess of marriage and family. Hera was associated with the cow and the peacock — both celestial symbols in ancient iconography. Ranked #582 in the 2025 U.S. list.

#### 63. Horizon
- **Meaning:** "Limit of sight," "The line where earth meets sky"
- **Origin:** Greek *horizon kyklos* (ὁρίζων κύκλος), "limiting circle"
- **Note:** A nature-inspired name with cosmic implications. The horizon is the boundary between the known and the unknown — a concept that resonates deeply in an era of space exploration. Used primarily in English-speaking countries.

#### 64. Kepler
- **Meaning:** "Cobbler" or "Weather prophet"
- **Origin:** German *Kepler*, from *Keppler* (cobbler)
- **Note:** Johannes Kepler discovered that planets move in elliptical orbits, not perfect circles. Kepler's laws of planetary motion are foundational to orbital mechanics. The name has gained popularity in the 2010s and 2020s, ranking #589 in the 2025 U.S. list.

#### 65. Lyra
- **Meaning:** "Lyre"
- **Origin:** Greek *lyra*
- **Note:** Also listed under Constellation Names. The constellation Lyra is small but contains Vega, one of the most important stars in celestial navigation. The lyre was the instrument of Orpheus, whose music could charm all living things.

#### 66. Mercury
- **Meaning:** "Merchant," "Traveler"
- **Origin:** Latin *Mercurius*
- **Note:** Also listed under Planetary Names. Mercury the planet is the fastest-moving planet in the solar system, completing an orbit in just 88 Earth days. The Roman god Mercury was the messenger of the gods, associated with communication and travel.

#### 67. Nebula
- **Meaning:** "Cloud," "Mist"
- **Origin:** Latin *nebula*
- **Note:** A giant cloud of dust and gas in space — the birthplace of stars. The Orion Nebula (M42) is visible to the naked eye and is one of the most studied objects in astronomy. A name that is both poetic and scientifically precise.

#### 68. Perseus
- **Meaning:** "Slayer of beasts"
- **Origin:** Greek *Perseus*
- **Note:** Also listed under Constellation Names. The Perseusid meteor shower occurs every August when Earth passes through the debris of Swift-Tuttle's comet. A name associated with both heroism and celestial fireworks.

#### 69. Phoenix
- **Meaning:** "Purple-red" or "The Tyrian color"
- **Origin:** Greek *Phoinix* (Φοῖνιξ), from *phoinos* (blood-red)
- **Note:** The mythical bird that rises from its own ashes, also the name of a constellation in the southern sky. NASA's Phoenix Mars Lander touched down in 2008. A name of resurrection and renewal — thematically powerful for a child's identity.

#### 70. Sol
- **Meaning:** "Sun"
- **Origin:** Latin *Sol*
- **Note:** Also listed under Stellar Names. Sol is the star at the center of our solar system, and the name is used in scientific contexts to distinguish our Sun from stars in other systems. A minimalist name with maximum meaning.

### Astronomical Concepts and Deep-Sky Objects (15 Names)

These names come from the broader vocabulary of astronomy — galaxies, quasars, cosmic phenomena, and the mathematical concepts that describe them.

#### 71. Andromeda
- **Meaning:** "Ruler of men"
- **Origin:** Greek *Andromeda*
- **Note:** Also listed under Constellation Names. The Andromeda Galaxy (M31) is the nearest large galaxy to the Milky Way, visible to the naked eye under dark skies. It is on a collision course with our galaxy, expected in roughly 4.5 billion years.

#### 72. Apex
- **Meaning:** "Summit," "Peak," "The highest point"
- **Origin:** Latin *apex* (peak, summit)
- **Note:** The apex of the solar system's motion through the galaxy points toward a region near the constellation Hercules. A name that suggests reaching the highest possible point — both literally and metaphorically.

#### 73. Astro
- **Meaning:** "Star"
- **Origin:** Greek *astron*
- **Note:** Also listed under Stellar Names. The prefix *astro-* appears in dozens of scientific terms — astronomy, astrology, astronaut, astrophysics. As a standalone name, it is direct and modern.

#### 74. Comet
- **Meaning:** "Long-haired star"
- **Origin:** Greek *komētēs* (κομήτης), from *komē* (hair)
- **Note:** Comet nuclei are ancient remnants from the formation of the solar system. Halley's Comet, visible every 75-76 years, has been observed since at least 240 BCE. A name that evokes transience, beauty, and return.

#### 75. Cosmos
- **Meaning:** "Order," "Universe," "Adornment"
- **Origin:** Greek *kosmos* (κόσμος), originally meaning "order" or "arrangement"
- **Note:** The Greek word *kosmos* referred to the universe as an ordered system — the opposite of *chaos*. Carl Sagan's seminal 1980 television series and book *Cosmos* introduced the word to millions. A name of philosophical depth.

#### 76. Eclipse
- **Meaning:** "Abandonment"
- **Origin:** Greek *ekleipsis*
- **Note:** Also listed under Atmospheric Phenomena. An eclipse is a moment of darkness caused by one celestial body passing in front of another. The word's etymology — "to abandon" — carries a poetic melancholy that contrasts with its visual splendor.

#### 77. Galaxy
- **Meaning:** "Milky" (from *galaxias kyklos*, "milky circle")
- **Origin:** Greek *galaxias* (γαλαξίας), from *gala* ( milk)
- **Note:** Our Milky Way galaxy contains an estimated 100-400 billion stars. The name *Galaxy* has been used as a given name since the early 20th century, particularly in the American South. A name that is both scientific and accessible.

#### 78. Kepler
- **Meaning:** "Cobbler"
- **Origin:** German *Kepler*
- **Note:** Also listed under Atmospheric Phenomena. Johannes Kepler's laws of planetary motion remain fundamental to orbital mechanics. The Kepler space telescope discovered over 2,600 confirmed exoplanets between 2009 and 2018.

#### 79. Lyra
- **Meaning:** "Lyre"
- **Origin:** Greek *lyra*
- **Note:** Also listed under Stellar and Constellation Names. The Lyra constellation is one of the 48 Ptolemaic constellations and is visible from both the Northern and Southern Hemispheres. The name's musical associations add a layer of cultural richness.

#### 80. Meteor
- **Meaning:** "Atmospheric," "Of the sky"
- **Origin:** Greek *meteōros* (μετέωρος), from *meteōr* (high in the air)
- **Note:** A meteor — commonly called a "shooting star" — is the luminous path produced when a meteoroid enters Earth's atmosphere. The Perseid meteor shower produces up to 100 meteors per hour. A name that captures a moment of brilliance.

#### 81. Nova
- **Meaning:** "New"
- **Origin:** Latin *nova*
- **Note:** Also listed under Stellar Names. A nova is a transient astronomical event that causes a star's brightness to increase dramatically. Unlike a supernova, a nova is reversible — the star survives and can produce multiple outbursts. A name of renewal and reinvention.

#### 82. Orion
- **Meaning:** "Rising in the sky"
- **Origin:** Greek *Orion*
- **Note:** Also listed under Stellar and Constellation Names. The Orion Nebula (M42) is one of the most studied objects in astronomy — a stellar nursery where new stars are being born. The name connects to both the hunter of myth and the cradle of stars.

#### 83. Pegasus
- **Meaning:** "Flash," "Swift"
- **Origin:** Greek *Pegasus*
- **Note:** Also listed under Constellation Names. The Pegasus space telescope concept (2006-2011) was designed to search for Earth-sized exoplanets. The constellation Pegasus contains the Great Square, one of the most useful navigation markers in the night sky.

#### 84. Polaris
- **Meaning:** "The North Star"
- **Origin:** Latin *polaris*, from *polus* (pole)
- **Note:** Polaris, also known as the North Star or Pole Star, has been used for navigation for thousands of years. It is currently within half a degree of the North Celestial Pole, making it the closest bright star to that point. A name synonymous with guidance and constancy.

#### 85. Quasar
- **Meaning:** "Quasi-stellar radio source"
- **Origin:** Coined by Chinese-American astronomer Hong-Yi Chuy in 1964, from *quasi-stellar*
- **Note:** Quasars are the brightest objects in the universe, powered by supermassive black holes at the centers of distant galaxies. A quasar can outshine an entire galaxy of trillions of stars. A name that is both scientific and extraordinary.

#### 86. Retro
- **Meaning:** "Backward" (from *retrograde*)
- **Origin:** Latin *retro* (backward) + *grade* (step)
- **Note:** Retrograde motion describes the apparent backward movement of a planet in the sky, as observed from Earth. The term has entered popular culture through astrology, but in astronomy it refers to a real and well-understood orbital phenomenon. A name with a modern, edgy feel.

#### 87. Sol
- **Meaning:** "Sun"
- **Origin:** Latin *Sol*
- **Note:** Also listed under Stellar and Atmospheric Names. In astronomy, *Sol* is used to distinguish our Sun from other stars. The name appears in science fiction — from *2001: A Space Odyssey* to *The Expanse* — as a shorthand for the home star of humanity.

#### 88. Stella
- **Meaning:** "Star"
- **Origin:** Latin *stella*
- **Note:** Also listed under Stellar Names. *Stella* has been used as a given name since the Middle Ages and appears in literature from Chaucer to Shakespeare. The name's simplicity and universality make it one of the most recognizable celestial names in the world.

#### 89. Zenith
- **Meaning:** "The point directly overhead"
- **Origin:** Arabic *samt ar-ra's* (سمت الرأس), "direction of the head"
- **Note:** The zenith is the highest point in the sky, directly above an observer. The word entered English via Old French *zénith* from the Arabic. A name that suggests reaching the highest possible point — both in the sky and in life.

#### 90. Zodiac
- **Meaning:** "Circle of animals"
- **Origin:** Greek *zōidiakos kyklos* (ζωδιακός κύκλος), from *zōidion* (little animal)
- **Note:** The zodiac is a belt of 12 constellations through which the Sun, Moon, and planets appear to move. While astrology has a complex cultural history, the zodiac constellations are real astronomical objects with names that have been used for thousands of years. A name that is bold and distinctive.

### Additional Stellar and Cosmic Names (10 Names)

These final names round out the collection with additional stellar, lunar, and cosmic options.

#### 91. Althea
- **Meaning:** "Healing," "Wholesome"
- **Origin:** Greek *Althea* (Ἀλθέα), from *altheia* (healing)
- **Note:** A star in the constellation Lyra (also known as Beta Lyrae). Althea has been used as a given name since the Renaissance and carries a gentle, medicinal resonance.

#### 92. Altair
- **Meaning:** "The Flying One"
- **Origin:** Arabic *al-tair*
- **Note:** Also listed under Stellar Names. Altair is the brightest star in Aquila and one of the vertices of the Summer Triangle. It rotates so rapidly that its equator bulges significantly — a fact discovered through interferometric observations.

#### 93. Ariel
- **Meaning:** "Lion of God"
- **Origin:** Hebrew *Ari'el* (אַרְיֵאל), from *ari* (lion) + *el* (God)
- **Note:** One of Uranus's major moons, discovered by James Kerr in 1851. Ariel is also a character in Shakespeare's *The Tempest* — a spirit associated with air and freedom. Ranked #547 in the 2025 U.S. list.

#### 94. Atlas
- **Meaning:** "To endure"
- **Origin:** Greek *Atlas*
- **Note:** Also listed under Planetary Names. Atlas is both a constellation and a moon of Saturn. The name carries the weight of the Titan who was condemned to hold up the heavens — a myth that has been reinterpreted through the centuries as a symbol of strength and endurance.

#### 95. Calypso
- **Meaning:** "She who hides"
- **Origin:** Greek *Kalypso* (Κάλυψις)
- **Note:** Also listed under Planetary Names. Calypso is one of Saturn's smallest known moons, discovered in 2007. The name's mythological associations — the nymph who detained Odysseus for seven years — add a layer of narrative depth.

#### 96. Cosmos
- **Meaning:** "Order," "Universe"
- **Origin:** Greek *kosmos*
- **Note:** Also listed under Astronomical Concepts. The ancient Greek concept of *kosmos* as an ordered system — as opposed to *chaos
