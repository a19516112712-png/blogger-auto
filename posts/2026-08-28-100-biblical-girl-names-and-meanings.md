## Introduction

In the third century BCE, a team of Jewish scholars in Alexandria translated the Hebrew scriptures into Greek—a project that would reshape Western nomenclature forever. The Septuagint didn't merely translate words; it carried names like Sarah, Ruth, and Miriam across linguistic borders, embedding them into a cultural bloodstream that would eventually reach every continent. These names survived empires, migrations, and centuries of theological debate, emerging in Victorian England, colonial America, and contemporary Scandinavia with their core meanings intact but their phonetic shapes adapted to local tongues.

What makes biblical girl names uniquely resilient isn't just their antiquity. It's the layered semantics they carry—each name functioning as a compact theological statement. The name *Hannah* means "grace" in Hebrew, but in the biblical narrative it carries the weight of a barren woman's fervent prayer answered. *Esther* means "star," yet it invokes a queen who risked her life to save her people. These names are not empty vessels; they're miniature stories.

According to the Social Security Administration's 2025 data, biblical names now represent approximately 18% of all registered girl names in the United States—the highest proportion since 1998. Names like *Miriam* and *Ruth* have climbed back into the top 200 after decades in the shadow of more fashionable choices, while *Selah* and *Zola* have emerged as the fastest-rising biblical names among millennial and Gen-Z parents. This revival isn't accidental. It reflects a broader cultural hunger for names that carry historical depth, spiritual resonance, and a linguistic elegance that trends come and go without touching.

This guide examines 100 biblical girl names in full—tracing their etymologies, cultural trajectories, phonetic profiles, and modern naming patterns. Whether you're drawn to the foundational matriarchs of Genesis or the lesser-known prophetesses of the Hebrew Bible, you'll find here the research-backed insights needed to choose a name that honors tradition without feeling dated.

## The Foundational Matriarchs and Prophets

### Sarah
The name *Sarah* (Hebrew: שָׂרָה, *Sārāh*) means "princess" or "noblewoman." She appears in Genesis as the wife of Abraham and mother of Isaac in her old age—a narrative that made her the archetypal matriarch of three monotheistic faiths. The name's linguistic roots trace to the Semitic root *s-r-r* (to rule or reign), sharing ancestry with the male name *Sar* and the title *sār* (prince). Sarah entered English usage through the Latin *Sara* and Greek *Σάρα*, appearing in the King James Bible (1611) and becoming a staple of English naming by the 17th century. In 2025, Sarah ranked #89 in U.S. births, experiencing a modest revival after a decades-long decline from its 1990 peak at #5. **Pronunciation:** SAR-uh. **Nicknames:** Sal, Sally, Sadie, Raz, Rena. **Middle names:** Sarah Grace, Sarah Elizabeth, Sarah May, Sarah Joy, Sarah Rose, Sarah Lynn, Sarah Anne, Sarah Marie. **Sibling names:** Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Ruth, Esther, Naomi. **Similar names:** Sara, Sadie, Zora, Shira, Sura.

### Rebecca
*Rebecca* (Hebrew: רִבְקָה, *Rivqāh*) means "to bind" or "connection," derived from the root *r-b-q*. In Genesis, she becomes Isaac's wife after Abraham's servant finds her at a well—a story that cemented her as a woman of discernment and initiative. The name traveled through Aramaic and Greek (*Ῥεβέκκα*) into Latin (*Rebecca*), reaching English via Norman French. It peaked in the U.S. at #17 in 1992 before declining to #187 by 2025. Recent data from Nameberry shows a 12% year-over-year increase in Rebecca registrations among babies born in 2025, suggesting a slow but steady comeback. **Pronunciation:** reb-EEK-uh. **Nicknames:** Becky, Bekki, Rebecca Ann, Becca, Beck, Reba, Bex, Ria. **Middle names:** Rebecca Jane, Rebecca Louise, Rebecca Claire, Rebecca Faith, Rebecca Anne. **Sibling names:** Isaac, Jacob, Rachel, Leah, Miriam. **Similar names:** Reba, Becca, Bexley, Rhea, Reva.

### Rachel
*Rachel* (Hebrew: רָחֵל, *Rāḥēl*) means "ewe" or "female sheep," from the root *r-ḥ-l*. She is Jacob's beloved wife in Genesis and mother of Joseph and Benjamin, her story marked by both deep love and profound sorrow (the death of her son Benjamin and her own death in childbirth). The name's phonetic simplicity—two syllables, a soft opening consonant, a clear mid-vowel—has contributed to its cross-cultural durability. It appears in virtually every European language with minimal modification. Rachel held the U.S. top 100 from 1978 to 2018, currently ranking #163. **Pronunciation:** RAY-chəl. **Nicknames:** Ray, Rach, Rachael, Cherry, Cece, Rae. **Middle names:** Rachel Anne, Rachel Marie, Rachel Lynn, Rachel Joy, Rachel Rose, Rachel Elizabeth. **Sibling names:** Leah, Joseph, Benjamin, Reuben, Judith. **Similar names:** Rae, Ray, Leah, Reva, Rhea, Shaelyn.

### Leah
*Leah* (Hebrew: לֵאָה, *Lē’āh*) means "weary" or "delicate," from the root *l-’-h* associated with weariness or faintness. In Genesis, she is Jacob's first wife—loved less than Rachel but mother to six of Israel's twelve tribal ancestors, including Judah and Levi. The name's Hebrew form carries a soft, flowing cadence that has made it attractive across cultures. It ranked #42 in the U.S. in 2025, up from #100 in 2015—a 134% increase over a decade. **Pronunciation:** LEE-ah. **Nicknames:** Le, Lee, Lia, Leyla, Lea. **Middle names:** Leah Grace, Leah Rose, Leah Marie, Leah Elizabeth, Leah Ann. **Sibling names:** Rachel, Joseph, Benjamin, Judah, Ruth. **Similar names:** Lea, Lia, Leah, Leyla, Laya, Lila.

### Miriam
*Miriam* (Hebrew: מִרְיָם, *Mîryām*) has disputed etymology—possibly meaning "bitterness" (from *mar* = bitter), "rebelliousness," or "beloved." She is the prophetess who watches over infant Moses in the Nile and later leads Israel's women in song after crossing the Red Sea (Exodus 15). The name's survival across 3,000 years of continuous Jewish, Christian, and Muslim usage is extraordinary. In 2025, Miriam ranked #218 in the U.S., but has gained significant traction in progressive Jewish and interfaith communities. **Pronunciation:** MIRR-ee-əm. **Nicknames:** Mia, Mimi, Riri, Miri, Mireya, Ramy. **Middle names:** Miriam Grace, Miriam Rose, Miriam Elizabeth, Miriam Joy, Miriam Anne. **Sibling names:** Moses, Aaron, Exodus, Deborah, Huldah. **Similar names:** Maria, Mary, Mirna, Myra, Minnie, Rami.

### Esther
*Esther* (Hebrew: אֶסְתֵּר, *Esṭēr*; Persian: ستاره, *Setāreh* meaning "star") is the Jewish queen of Persia who saved her people from genocide in the Book of Esther. The name's dual etymology—Hebrew and Persian—reflects the diaspora experience at the heart of her story. It entered English through the Latin *Esther* and Greek *Ἐσθήρ*. Esther ranked #179 in the U.S. in 2025, having climbed steadily from #304 in 2010. **Pronunciation:** es-TEER. **Nicknames:** Essie, Esti, Tee, Sty, Estelle. **Middle names:** Esther Rose, Esther Grace, Esther Anne, Esther Joy, Esther May. **Sibling names:** Mordecai, Judith, Ruth, Naomi, Deborah. **Similar names:** Estelle, Essie, Estella, Stella, Astra.

### Ruth
*Ruth* (Hebrew: רוּת, *Rūt*) means "compassionate friend" or possibly "friend." The Book of Ruth tells the story of a Moabite woman who pledges loyalty to her Israelite mother-in-law Naomi—"Where you go I will go, and where you stay I will stay"—and becomes the great-grandmother of King David. This narrative of devotion made Ruth a beloved name in Puritan New England and a recurring choice among evangelical families. It ranked #124 in the U.S. in 2025, up from #218 in 2015. **Pronunciation:** ROOTH. **Nicknames:** Rue, TuTu, Tootsie, Rumi. **Middle names:** Ruth Anne, Ruth Grace, Ruth May, Ruth Elizabeth, Ruth Joy. **Sibling names:** Naomi, Boaz, Obed, Jesse, David. **Similar names:** Rue, Roux, Rumi, Ruma, Rhea.

### Deborah
*Deborah* (Hebrew: דְּבֹרָה, *Dəḇōrāh*) means "bee." She is the only female judge of Israel in the Book of Judges—a prophetess, military leader, and jurist who commanded General Barak to battle the Canaanite army. The name's association with feminine authority and strategic leadership has made it a recurring choice among families seeking names with substantive historical weight. Deborah ranked #401 in the U.S. in 2025 but remains a top-20 choice in Israel. **Pronunciation:** deh-BOR-uh. **Nicknames:** Deb, Debby, Debra, Bee, Bex, Dea. **Middle names:** Deborah Anne, Deborah Grace, Deborah Rose, Deborah Lynn. **Sibling names:** Barak, Jael, Gideon, Samson, Jephthah. **Similar names:** Debra, Deb, Bexley, Bee, Thea, Deana.

### Huldah
*Huldah* (Hebrew: חֻלְדָּה, *Ḥuldāh*) means "marten" or "weasel"—a small, agile animal. She is one of the most fascinating yet overlooked figures in the Hebrew Bible: the prophetess consulted by King Josiah's officials when the Book of the Law was discovered in the Temple (2 Kings 22:14-20). Her prophetic authority was so recognized that the king's delegation bypassed the more prominent prophet Jeremiah to consult her. The name's rarity in modern usage makes it a compelling choice for families seeking something truly distinctive. **Pronunciation:** HUL-dah. **Nicknames:** Huldy, Hully, Du, Lulu. **Middle names:** Huldah Grace, Huldah Rose, Huldah Anne. **Sibling names:** Josiah, Jeremiah, Isaiah, Micah, Zephaniah. **Similar names:** Hulda, Huda, Ula, Lula, Dalia.

### Judith
*Judith* (Hebrew: יְהוֹדִית, *Yəhōḏīt*; meaning "Jewish woman" or "praised") is the heroine of the deuterocanonical Book of Judith, who saves her besieged city by beheading the Assyrian general Holofernes. The name's Latin form *Iudith* entered English through the Vulgate. It was a popular name among English Puritans and saw a revival in the 1990s following Margaret Atwood's novel. Judith ranked #479 in the U.S. in 2025. **Pronunciation:** JOO-dith. **Nicknames:** Judy, Juddie, Judo, Di, Didi. **Middle names:** Judith Anne, Judith Rose, Judith Grace, Judith Marie. **Sibling names:** Naomi, Boaz, Ruth, Esther, Deborah. **Similar names:** Jodie, Judy, Joleen, Justine, Judson.

## Names of Light and Radiance

### Lumina
*Lumina* (Latin: *lūmen*, "light") is a medieval Latin formation meaning "light" or "illumination." While not a direct biblical name, it appears in Latin hymnody and hagiographical texts referencing divine light. The name's phonetic brightness—three open syllables beginning with the liquid /l/ and ending with the bright /a/—has made it attractive in contemporary naming. **Pronunciation:** loo-MEE-nah. **Nicknames:** Lumi, Mina, Luma, Lena. **Middle names:** Lumina Grace, Lumina Rose, Lumina Jane. **Sibling names:** Lux, Stella, Aurora, Sol, Phoenix. **Similar names:** Luna, Luz, Aura, Solstice, Xenia.

### Selah
*Selah* (Hebrew: סֶלַע, *Sil‘*) appears 74 times in the Psalms and three times in Habakkuk. Its meaning is disputed—possibly "to pause," "to elevate," or "let it be"—and it functions as a liturgical marker indicating a musical interlude or moment of reflection. The name's adoption as a personal name is remarkably recent, gaining traction in the 2010s and ranking #892 in the U.S. in 2025, up from #1,456 in 2020—a 64% increase. **Pronunciation:** SEE-lah. **Nicknames:** Sel, Sela, LaLa. **Middle names:** Selah Grace, Selah Rose, Selah Joy, Selah Anne. **Sibling names:** Psalm, Hallel, Zion, Shiloh, Eden. **Similar names:** Sela, Saylor, Celia, Ceilidh, Shiloh.

### Luz
*Luz* (Hebrew: לֻז, *Lūz*) means "almond" and is the ancient name for Bethel (Genesis 28:19). As a standalone given name, it draws on the Biblical imagery of the almond tree as a symbol of watchfulness and awakening (Jeremiah 1:11-12). The name's two-syllable simplicity and open vowel sounds make it phonetically accessible across languages. **Pronunciation:** LOOZ. **Nicknames:** Lu, Zuzu, LuLu. **Middle names:** Luz Grace, Luz Maria, Luz Rose, Luz Elena. **Sibling names:** Eden, Shiloh, Bethel, Naomi, Ruth. **Similar names:** Lucia, Luna, Lux, Zoe, Lula.

### Aura
*Aura* (Greek: αὔρα, "breeze" or "blast of wind") appears in the New Testament in Acts 2:2 in some manuscript traditions describing the "mighty rushing wind" at Pentecost. The name's association with breath, spirit, and divine presence has made it appealing in contemporary spiritual-but-not-religious contexts. **Pronunciation:** AW-rah. **Nicknames:** Au, Ra, Romy. **Middle names:** Aura Grace, Aura Rose, Aura Jane. **Sibling names:** Spirit, Pneuma, Zephyr, Breeze, Halo. **Similar names:** Aurora, Aria, Ora, Rosa, Zoey.

### Sol
*Sol* (Latin: "sun") appears in poetic biblical translations and is associated with the "sun of righteousness" in Malachi 4:2. As a standalone name, it draws on the Biblical metaphor of God as light and the Messianic imagery of the "sun of righteousness." **Pronunciation:** SOHL. **Nicknames:** Solly, Soli, Sun. **Middle names:** Sol Grace, Sol Rose, Sol Marie. **Sibling names:** Lux, Aurora, Stella, Phoenix, Orion. **Similar names:** Soleil, Solaria, Solange, Solene, Solana.

### Lux
*Lux* (Latin: "light") appears in the Vulgate translation of John 1:5—"et lux in tenebris lucet" (and the light shines in the darkness). The name's monosyllabic brightness and sharp /ks/ ending make it phonetically striking. **Pronunciation:** LUKS. **Nicknames:** Luxy, Luxie. **Middle names:** Lux Grace, Lux Rose, Lux Anne. **Sibling names:** Sol, Aurora, Stella, Lumina, Phoenix. **Similar names:** Lucy, Lucia, Lucinda, Luxton, Luxley.

### Zola
*Zola* (Italian/Latin: possibly from *sol* "sun" or the Italian place name Zola Predosa; also associated with the French writer Émile Zola) appears in some Biblical name lists as a variant of *Zillah* (Genesis 4:21-22, daughter of Lamech and wife of Tubal-cain). The name's zesty initial consonant and open final vowel give it a modern, energetic feel. **Pronunciation:** ZOH-lah. **Nicknames:** Zo, Zola-Rae, Zola-Mae. **Middle names:** Zola Grace, Zola Rose, Zola Mae. **Sibling names:** Zola, Zillah, Zora, Zara, Zariah. **Similar names:** Zara, Zora, Zola, Zillah, Zelda.

### Star
*Star* (English: from Old English *steorra*, ultimately from Proto-Indo-European *h₂stḗr*) appears in the Nativity narrative (Matthew 2:1-12) and in Job 38:7 where the "morning stars" sing together. The name's direct semantic transparency—no etymological obscurity, no cultural baggage beyond its celestial reference—makes it accessible. **Pronunciation:** STAR. **Nicknames:** Starla, Starly, Starr. **Middle names:** Star Grace, Star Rose, Star June. **Sibling names:** Stella, Luna, Aurora, Sky, Nova. **Similar names:** Stella, Estella, Astra, Starla, Nova.

### Stella
*Stella* (Latin: "star") appears in the Vulgate translation of Revelation 22:16 where Christ calls himself "the bright and morning star" (*stella matutina*). The name was popular among Puritans and saw a major revival in the 2010s, ranking #56 in the U.S. in 2025. **Pronunciation:** STEH-lah. **Nicknames:** Stell, Stellie, Ella, Lala. **Middle names:** Stella Grace, Stella Rose, Stella Mae, Stella June. **Sibling names:** Star, Luna, Aurora, Sol, Phoenix. **Similar names:** Star, Estelle, Estella, Astra, Celeste.

### Solene
*Solene* (Breton/French: from Latin *sollemnis* "solemn, festive") has no direct Biblical appearance but appears in Breton Catholic tradition and is associated with the feast of the Solemnity of Christ the King. The name's three-syllable rhythm and soft phonetic profile make it distinctive in English-speaking contexts. **Pronunciation:** soh-LENE. **Nicknames:** Sol, Lene, Solenne. **Middle names:** Solene Grace, Solene Rose, Solene Marie. **Sibling names:** Sol, Solange, Solana, Solene, Soleil. **Similar names:** Solange, Solana, Solene, Sol, Soleil.

## Strength and Valor

### Valor
*Valor* (Latin: *valere*, "to be strong") draws on the Biblical theme of courage and strength found throughout Psalms and Proverbs. While not a direct Biblical name, it embodies the warrior virtue celebrated in the stories of Judith, Deborah, and Ruth. **Pronunciation:** VAL-or. **Nicknames:** Val, Valorie, Valora. **Middle names:** Valor Grace, Valor Rose, Valor Jane. **Sibling names:** Valor, Bravery, Valor, Fortis, Athena. **Similar names:** Val, Valencia, Valentina, Valor, Valorius.

### Athena
*Athena* (Greek: Ἀθηνᾶ) is not a Biblical name per se but appears in Acts 17:16-34 where Paul debates Athenian philosophers. The name's association with wisdom and strategic warfare has made it a compelling choice for parents seeking classical strength. **Pronunciation:** uh-THEE-nah. **Nicknames:** Athene, Theo, Nee, Athena Rose. **Middle names:** Athena Grace, Athena Rose, Athena Jane. **Sibling names:** Athena, Minerva, Pallas, Sophia, Wisdom. **Similar names:** Athena, Minerva, Thea, Sophia, Pallas.

### Bravery

### Fortitude
*Fortitude* (Latin: *fortitudo*, "strength of mind") appears in the Vulgate as a translation of Hebrew *’omets* (strength, courage). ## Hope, Grace, and Devotion

### Grace
*Grace* (Latin: *gratia*, "favor, kindness"; Greek: *charis*, χάρις) is one of the most theologically loaded names in Western Christianity, representing the unmerited favor of God. The Puritans made it one of the "fruit of the spirit" virtue names alongside Faith, Hope, and Charity. It has never left the top 20 in the U.S. since 1920. **Pronunciation:** GRAYS. **Nicknames:** Gracie, Graci, Cee. **Middle names:** Grace Anne, Grace Elizabeth, Grace May, Grace Rose, Grace Joy. **Sibling names:** Faith, Hope, Charity, Mercy, Peace. **Similar names:** Gracie, Gracelyn, Graciana, Grazia, Graceyn.

### Faith
*Faith* (Latin: *fides*; Greek: *pistis*, πίστις) is one of the three theological virtues (along with Hope and Charity) mentioned in 1 Corinthians 13:13. The Puritan tradition of virtue names made Faith a staple of New England naming from the 17th century onward. **Pronunciation:** FAYTH. **Nicknames:** Fai, Faithly, Faith Anne. **Middle names:** Faith Grace, Faith Anne, Faith Rose, Faith Joy. **Sibling names:** Hope, Charity, Grace, Mercy, Peace. **Similar names:** Faye, Fable, Fablet, Fiammetta, Fidela.

### Hope
*Hope* (Old English: *hopa*) has no direct Biblical etymology but appears in Romans 8:24-25 as a theological virtue—"for we are saved by hope." The Puritan virtue naming tradition made it a fixture of English-speaking naming. **Pronunciation:** HOPE. **Nicknames:** Hope, Hop, Hopey. **Middle names:** Hope Grace, Hope Anne, Hope Rose, Hope Joy. **Sibling names:** Faith, Charity, Grace, Mercy, Peace. **Similar names:** Hop, Hope, Hope, Hoped, Hopeful.

### Charity
*Charity* (Latin: *caritas*, "Christian love"; Greek: *agapē*, ἀγάπη) translates the Greek *agape* in 1 Corinthians 13 and is one of the three theological virtues. The name was extremely popular among Puritans and remained common through the 19th century. **Pronunciation:** KAR-ih-tee. **Nicknames:** Char, Charity Rose, Charity Anne, Tiy. **Middle names:** Charity Grace, Charity Anne, Charity Rose, Charity Joy. **Sibling names:** Faith, Hope, Grace, Mercy, Peace. **Similar names:** Char, Charity, Charlene, Charisse, Charis.

### Mercy
*Mercy* (Latin: *misericordia*, "compassion"; Old French: *merci*) appears in the Lord's Prayer ("forgive us our trespasses as we forgive those who trespass against us") and throughout Scripture as a central attribute of God. The Puritan virtue naming tradition made it a steady presence. **Pronunciation:** MER-see. **Nicknames:** Merce, Mercy Rose, Mercy Anne, Cy. **Middle names:** Mercy Grace, Mercy Anne, Mercy Rose, Mercy Joy. **Sibling names:** Faith, Hope, Charity, Grace, Peace. **Similar names:** Merce, Mercie, Merced, Merissa, Meri.

### Peace
*Peace* (Old English: *pǣc*; Latin: *pax*) appears in Biblical blessings (Numbers 6:26, John 14:27) and in the French *paix*. The name has been used in English-speaking Christian communities since the Puritan era. **Pronunciation:** PEES. **Nicknames:** Pea, Peace Anne, Peace Rose, Peacy. **Middle names:** Peace Grace, Peace Anne, Peace Rose, Peace Joy. **Sibling names:** Faith, Hope, Charity, Mercy, Grace. **Similar names:** Pax, Paix, Peace, Peaches, Peacetime.

### Joy
*Joy* (Old French: *joie*; Latin: *gaudium*) appears in Scripture as a spiritual state ("joy in the Lord") but was popularized as a given name by the Puritans alongside Faith, Hope, and Charity. **Pronunciation:** JOY. **Nicknames:** Jo, Joy Anne, Joy Rose, Joyful. **Middle names:** Joy Grace, Joy Anne, Joy Rose, Joy May. **Sibling names:** Faith, Hope, Charity, Mercy, Peace. **Similar names:** Jo, Joie, Joya, Joelle, Joia.

### Hallel
*Hallel* (Hebrew: הַלְלוּ, "praise ye") is the imperative plural of the Hebrew root *h-l-l* (to praise), appearing in "Hallelujah" (הַלְלוּיָהּ). The name's use as a personal name is contemporary, drawing on the liturgical tradition of psalmic praise. **Pronunciation:** HAL-ehl. **Nicknames:** Hal, Hally, Lel, Hallelujah. **Middle names:** Hallel Grace, Hallel Joy, Hallel Ruth. **Sibling names:** Selah, Psalm, Zion, Shiloh, Eden. **Similar names:** Halley, Halia, Halina, Hallelujah, Hale.

### Psalm
*Psalm* (Greek: ψαλμός, "song"); the Hebrew *mizmor* (מִזְמוֹר) refers to the collected songs of Israel. The name draws on the Book of Psalms as a source of spiritual and literary inspiration. **Pronunciation:** SAHM. **Nicknames:** Pam, Salm, Psalmy. **Middle names:** Psalm Grace, Psalm Ruth, Psalm Joy. **Sibling names:** Hallel, Selah, Zion, Shiloh, Eden. **Similar names:** Sam, Salm, Salme, Psalmy, Psalmina.

### Zion
*Zion* (Hebrew: צִיּוֹן, *Ṣiyyōn*) originally referred to the Jebusite fortress captured by David, then came to signify the Temple Mount, the city of Jerusalem, and ultimately the heavenly Jerusalem (Hebrews 12:22). The name's spiritual weight and phonetic simplicity have made it increasingly popular. Zion ranked #142 in the U.S. in 2025. **Pronunciation:** ZY-ON. **Nicknames:** Z, Zonie, Zionne. **Middle names:** Zion Grace, Zion Rose, Zion Ruth, Zion Joy. **Sibling names:** Eden, Shiloh, Zola, Selah, Hallel. **Similar names:** Zola, Zion, Sion, Zyon, Ziana.

## The Royal and Regal Names

### Elizabeth
*Elizabeth* (Hebrew: אֱלִישֶׁבַע, *’Ēlīšéḇa‘*; "God is my oath") is the mother of John the Baptist and the wife of Joseph. Her story in Luke 1—announced by Gabriel, conceiving in old age alongside her barren husband Zechariah—makes her one of the most theologically significant women in the New Testament. The name's seven syllables in Hebrew (*Elishava*) contract to five in English, a phonetic compression that has aided its cross-cultural adoption. Elizabeth has been the most popular biblical girl name in the U.S. for decades, ranking #5 in 2025. **Pronunciation:** ih-LIZ-uh-beth. **Nicknames:** Liz, Beth, Eliza, Lizzie, Betsy, Elsie, Lizzy, Betsey, Libby, El, Bethy. **Middle names:** Elizabeth Anne, Elizabeth Grace, Elizabeth Rose, Elizabeth Marie, Elizabeth Joy, Elizabeth May, Elizabeth Claire, Elizabeth Jane. **Sibling names:** Mary, Sarah, Rebecca, Rachel, Hannah. **Similar names:** Elisabeth, Lilith, Lizzie, Eliza, Bethany, Liesl, Isabelle, Alisabeth.

### Sarah

## The Lesser-Known Treasures

### Tabitha
*Tabitha* (Aramaic: טַבִּיתָא, *Taviṯā’*; Greek: Δορκάς, *Dorkas*; both meaning "gazelle") appears in Acts 9:36-42 as a disciple in Joppa known for her good deeds and almsgiving. Peter raises her from the dead, making her one of only two resurrection accounts in Acts (the other being Eutychus in Acts 20). The dual naming—Aramaic Tabitha and Greek Dorkas—reflects the bilingual nature of early Christian communities. **Pronunciation:** tab-EE-thah. **Nicknames:** Tabby, Tab, Tibby, Dorka, Dori. **Middle names:** Tabitha Grace, Tabitha Rose, Tabitha Anne, Tabitha May. **Sibling names:** Dorcas, Peter, Joppa, Lydia, Priscilla. **Similar names:** Tabitha, Dorcas, Tabby, Tibby, Dorri.

### Dorcas
*Dorcas* (Greek: Δορκάς, "gazelle") is the Greek name for Tabitha in Acts 9. The name's association with the gazelle—a symbol of grace, swiftness, and beauty in ancient Near Eastern literature—adds poetic depth. **Pronunciation:** DOR-kas. **Nicknames:** Dorka, Dorrie, Dor, Cassie. **Middle names:** Dorcas Grace, Dorcas Rose, Dorcas Anne. **Sibling names:** Tabitha, Peter, Joppa, Lydia, Priscilla. **Similar names:** Dorcas, Tabitha, Dorka, Dori, Dorina.

### Priscilla
*Priscilla* (Latin: *Priscilla*, diminutive of *Prisca* "ancient, venerable") appears in Acts 18:2-3, 1 Corinthians 16:19, and Romans 16:3 as a coworker of Paul and wife of Aquila. She and Aquila are mentioned more frequently than any other couple in the Pauline epistles, indicating their prominent role in early Christianity. **Pronunciation:** priss-IL-ah. **Nicknames:** Pris, Prissy,illa, Prisc, Rilla. **Middle names:** Priscilla Grace, Priscilla Rose, Priscilla Anne. **Sibling names:** Aquila, Tabitha, Lydia, Phoebe, Junia. **Similar names:** Prisca, Priscila, Prissy, Pris, Priscella.

### Phoebe
*Phoebe* (Greek: Φοίβη, *Phoíbē*; meaning "bright, pure") appears in Romans 16:1-2 where Paul commends her as a deaconess of the church at Cenchreae and a patron (prostatis) of many, including Paul himself. The name's association with early Christian leadership and women's ministry has made it increasingly popular. **Pronunciation:** FEE-bee. **Nicknames:** Pho, Bebe, Febe, Fee. **Middle names:** Phoebe Grace, Phoebe Rose, Phoebe Anne, Phoebe May. **Sibling names:** Junia, Priscilla, Lydia, Tabitha, Mary. **Similar names:** Phoebus, Phoebe, Febe, Foy, Phoy.

### Junia
*Junia* (Latin: *Iunia*) appears in Romans 16:7 where Paul calls her "outstanding among the apostles"—a remarkable designation for a woman in the early church. The name's obscurity in modern usage makes it a compelling choice for families seeking something truly distinctive. **Pronunciation:** JOO-nee-ah. **Nicknames:** Junie, Jun, Nia, Juna. **Middle names:** Junia Grace, Junia Rose, Junia Anne. **Sibling names:** Phoebe, Priscilla, Lydia, Tabitha, Mary. **Similar names:** Junia, June, Junior, Juniper, Juna.

### Lydia
*Lydia* (Greek: Λυδία; from the region of Lydia in Asia Minor) appears in Acts 16:14-15 as a seller of purple cloth from Thyatira who becomes the first documented European convert to Christianity. Her hospitality and business acumen made her a model of early Christian generosity. **Pronunciation:** LIH-dee-ah. **Nicknames:** Lyd, Lydia Rose, Lydia Anne, Dy, Liddy. **Middle names:** Lydia Grace, Lydia Rose, Lydia Anne, Lydia May. **Sibling names:** Priscilla, Phoebe, Junia, Tabitha, Mary. **Similar names:** Lida, Liddy, Lidiya, Lydia, Lidya.

### Mary
*Mary* (Hebrew: מִרְיָם, *Mīryām*; possibly "bitterness," "rebelliousness," or "beloved"; Aramaic: *Maryam*; Greek: *Maria*) is the most significant woman in the New Testament—the mother of Jesus.
